How these two axes fail differently

Speed degradationGradual and recoverable. Median detection time can slip from 4 min to about 8 min with no revenue impact; the loss starts (−27%) only when it passes the +30% red line (5.2 min) and Phase 1 triggers.
Accuracy degradationA cliff. Auto-detection falling from 90% to 85% doubles the manual-catch share (10% → 15%), which triggers Phase 2 and a −67% revenue hit. Frequent silent failures that humans have to catch deny the product's core value.
Auto-detection rateShare of confirmed failures that the system found itself. Rolling 4 h. Target ≥ 90%, red line < 85%.
Manual-catch fractionShare of confirmed failures first caught by a human report. Baseline 10%, red line 15%. This is the failure-monitoring metric for this screen.
Why a rising manual-catch can mean reviewer fatigueIf reviewers clear ambiguous alerts as “False alarm” to empty the queue, real failures stay live until a person in the field notices. Those show up here as manual catches.

Design rules enforced on this screen

  1. Three explicit outcomes only. There is no one-click pass, and every outcome needs a written note.
  2. Full evidence is always on screen: 45-min signal tracks, system reasoning for and against, and the complete memo history.
  3. Unresolved is not a way to close. It needs a re-check time and returns to the queue when it is due.
  4. Clearing a high-confidence (≥ 80%) detection as False alarm needs a second-source check with the officer or the footage.
  5. A fatigue guard warns when several fast False alarms are recorded in a row.
